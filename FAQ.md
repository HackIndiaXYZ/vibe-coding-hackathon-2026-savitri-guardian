# Frequently Asked Questions

## Why does Savitri exist?

Medical emergencies are rarely just medical problems.

People, families, responders, hospitals, and organizations often possess fragments of information, but nobody has the complete picture when decisions need to be made quickly.

Savitri exists to improve coordination during critical life events.

---

## What problem is Savitri solving?

The first emergency is often informational.

Who is this person?

Who should be contacted?

What information is important?

Who can help?

How do all stakeholders coordinate?

Savitri helps answer those questions quickly and responsibly.

---

## Can anyone scan my QR code?

Yes.

Anyone can scan a Savitri QR code.

However, scanning alone does not reveal medical information.

The QR code is designed to enable assistance, not unrestricted access.

---

## Can random people see my medical information?

No.

Before medical information can be viewed, a person must:

1. Acknowledge emergency access terms
2. Verify their phone number using OTP
3. Declare an emergency

These actions are recorded and audited.

Only the minimum information necessary to assist is disclosed.

---

## What prevents misuse?

Emergency access is not anonymous.

Reporter identity is verified through OTP verification and access is logged.

Notifications are sent to relevant stakeholders when emergency access occurs.

Future versions of Savitri will introduce abuse detection, risk scoring, and automated safeguards against repeated misuse.

---

## What if multiple people report the same emergency?

The long-term platform vision includes incident deduplication and evidence aggregation.

Multiple reports relating to the same person and incident will be merged into a single coordination workflow.

Additional reports become supporting evidence rather than creating duplicate emergencies.

This helps prevent notification fatigue and hospital overload.

---

## Does Savitri make medical decisions?

No.

Savitri does not diagnose, prescribe, triage, or make treatment decisions.

Medical decisions remain the responsibility of qualified healthcare professionals.

---

## Why use AI at all?

AI helps transform information into context.

Examples include:

* Incident summaries
* Voice note summarization
* Information consolidation
* Coordination support

AI assists decision-makers.

It does not replace them.

---

## What happens if AI is wrong?

AI outputs should be treated as assistance, not authority.

Humans remain responsible for reviewing, validating, and acting on information.

The appropriate level of automation should be determined by evidence, risk, and context.

---

## Who owns the data?

Individuals own their data.

Savitri's role is to help coordinate access, sharing, and communication.

The platform should store only what is necessary and federate information whenever practical.

---

## Why not store everything centrally?

Centralization increases risk, complexity, and responsibility.

Where possible, Savitri prefers a coordination model that connects information rather than becoming the system of record for everything.

The objective is trusted coordination, not data accumulation.

---

## Why not use the emergency information feature already available on smartphones?

Most smartphone emergency features are device-specific, ecosystem-specific, and focused on local access.

Savitri is designed around coordination.

It connects patients, families, public helpers, responders, hospitals, and organizations into a shared workflow rather than simply displaying information on a lock screen.

---

## Why allow public helpers to participate?

Because emergencies often begin with ordinary people.

The first person on the scene is rarely a doctor or paramedic.

Savitri seeks to reduce friction while maintaining accountability.

Anyone can help.

Not everyone receives the same access.

---

## How does Savitri make money if emergency assistance is free?

Emergency assistance should never depend on a person's ability to pay.

Revenue is expected to come from coordination infrastructure, integrations, enterprise deployments, and value-added services rather than emergency access itself.

---

## Is Savitri only for healthcare?

No.

Healthcare emergencies are the first implementation.

The broader vision is a Trusted Coordination Platform for Critical Life Events.

Future applications may include:

* Child safety
* Elder care
* Dementia support
* Missing persons
* Disaster response
* Community safety
* Caregiver coordination

---

## Why is the platform called Savitri?

Savitri is named after a legendary figure from Indian mythology.

According to the story, Savitri confronted Yama, the god of death, and through wisdom, determination, and devotion succeeded in bringing her husband back.

The story symbolizes courage, persistence, and refusing to give up on the people we care about.

Savitri's mission is not to defeat death.

Its mission is to help people coordinate, communicate, and act before it is too late.
