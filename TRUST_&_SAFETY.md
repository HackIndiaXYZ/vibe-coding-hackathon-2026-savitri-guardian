# Trust & Safety

> No emergency should begin with anonymity, uncertainty, or isolation.

Savitri is built on a simple belief:

The people who can help should have the information they need to help.

The people who cannot help should not have access to information they do not need.

Trust, privacy, accountability, and coordination are not competing goals. They must work together.

---

## 1. Discoverability With Dignity

People cannot receive assistance if they cannot be identified, located, or connected to the right helpers.

At the same time, discoverability should not require sacrificing privacy.

Savitri seeks to maximize a person's ability to receive assistance while exposing only the information necessary to enable that assistance.

---

## 2. Progressive Trust

Trust is earned, not assumed.

Different actions require different levels of trust, verification, accountability, and authorization.

Viewing a public profile requires less trust than acting on someone's behalf.

Updating medical information requires less trust than making decisions for another person.

Trust should increase as responsibility increases.

---

## 3. Verification Is Evidence, Not Trust

Verification helps establish confidence.

Trust determines what actions are permitted.

A verified stranger is not necessarily more trusted than an unverified parent.

Verification contributes to trust but does not replace it.

Savitri evaluates identity, relationships, verification, consent, accountability, and context when determining access and authority.

---

## 4. Privacy With Purpose

Medical information is among the most sensitive information a person possesses.

It should not be publicly accessible.

It should also not remain hidden when its absence could create harm.

Savitri balances privacy and assistance by ensuring that information is shared only when there is a legitimate reason, an appropriate level of trust, and a meaningful need to know.

---

## 5. Minimum Necessary Disclosure

Different participants require different information.

A bystander, family member, EMT, physician, caregiver, and hospital administrator do not need identical access.

Savitri follows a simple principle:

Share the minimum information necessary to achieve the intended outcome.

Nothing more.

Nothing less.

---

## 6. Consent Matters

Permissions explain what can happen.

Consent explains why it can happen.

Consent should be explicit whenever practical and may be:

* Granted
* Delegated
* Time-bound
* Limited
* Revoked

Individuals should remain in control of how they participate in the ecosystem.

---

## 7. Accountability Matters

Important actions should never be anonymous.

Emergency access, information disclosure, status changes, and other significant actions should be attributable, auditable, and reviewable.

If something happened, there should be a record.

Accountability creates trust.

---

## 8. Representation Matters

People are not always able to act for themselves.

Children, older adults, individuals with dementia, incapacitated patients, and other vulnerable populations may require trusted representatives.

Savitri is designed around a fundamental question:

Who can act for whom, under what circumstances, and with what authority?

Representation is treated as a first-class concept rather than an exception.

---

## 9. Context Before Action

Information alone rarely improves outcomes.

Information must be transformed into context, understanding, and informed decision-making.

Savitri's purpose is not to collect information.

Its purpose is to help people understand what is happening and what action is most appropriate.

---

## 10. Evidence-Based Automation

Automation should earn responsibility.

Decision authority should be delegated according to demonstrated reliability, risk, regulatory requirements, and real-world performance.

Some decisions should remain human.

Some decisions may be assisted by AI.

Some decisions may eventually be automated.

The appropriate level of automation should be determined by evidence, not ideology.

---

## Our Guiding Principle

The purpose of Savitri is not to collect information.

The purpose of Savitri is to help the right people receive the right information at the right time so they can take the right action.
