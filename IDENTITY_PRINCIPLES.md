# Identity Principles

> Identity is not just who a person is.
>
> Identity is also who can find them, help them, represent them, and act on their behalf.

Savitri is built on the belief that effective coordination begins with understanding people, relationships, responsibilities, and trust.

Identity is not a profile.

Identity is the foundation that enables coordination.

---

## 1. Identity Is More Than Identification

Most systems ask:

"Who are you?"

Savitri also asks:

* Who can help you?
* Who should be informed?
* Who can represent you?
* Who can make decisions when you cannot?
* Who is responsible for your care and safety?

Identity is not simply a name, phone number, or government ID.

Identity exists within relationships.

---

## 2. Identity Exists Beyond Applications

People exist independently of devices, applications, and service providers.

A person's identity should not disappear because:

* They changed phones
* They lost access to an account
* They changed providers
* They became incapacitated

Applications may change.

Identity should endure.

---

## 3. People Exist Within Networks

No person exists in isolation.

Every individual participates in a network that may include:

* Family
* Friends
* Caregivers
* Healthcare Providers
* Organizations
* Emergency Responders
* Public Helpers

Understanding these connections enables meaningful coordination when it matters most.

---

## 4. Relationships Are First-Class

Relationships are not metadata.

Relationships are part of the core model.

Examples include:

* Parent
* Child
* Guardian
* Caregiver
* Emergency Contact
* Physician
* Employer
* Volunteer

Relationships help determine who should know, who can help, and who can act.

---

## 5. Representation Matters

People are not always able to act for themselves.

Children, older adults, individuals with dementia, unconscious patients, and other vulnerable populations may require trusted representatives.

Representation answers a fundamental question:

> Who can act for me when I cannot act for myself?

Representation should be explicit, accountable, and appropriate to the situation.

---

## 6. Authority Is Contextual

Authority is not universal.

A person may have authority in one context and not another.

For example:

* A parent may make decisions for a child.
* An emergency responder may gain temporary authority during an active emergency.
* A physician may access information necessary for treatment.
* A caregiver may be authorized for specific activities.

Authority should always be linked to purpose and context.

---

## 7. Consent and Representation Are Different

Consent and representation are related but distinct.

Consent answers:

> What am I allowing?

Representation answers:

> Who may act for me?

A person may grant consent without granting representation.

A representative may have authority even when consent cannot be actively provided.

Both concepts are necessary for trusted coordination.

---

## 8. Verification Supports Trust

Verification helps establish confidence that a person, relationship, or organization is genuine.

Verification is important.

Trust is more important.

Verification provides evidence.

Trust determines authority.

Savitri evaluates identity, relationships, verification, consent, accountability, and context when determining access and responsibility.

---

## 9. Identity Enables Discoverability

People cannot receive assistance if they cannot be discovered by those capable of helping.

Discoverability answers questions such as:

* Who is this person?
* Who should be contacted?
* Who can help?
* Who is responsible?

The goal is not maximum visibility.

The goal is meaningful discoverability that enables assistance while preserving dignity and privacy.

---

## 10. Identity Enables Coordination

Every critical life event eventually becomes a coordination problem.

Medical emergencies.

Child safety.

Elder care.

Dementia support.

Missing persons.

Disaster response.

Identity determines who participates in that coordination and how they interact.

Without identity there can be no trust.

Without trust there can be no coordination.

---

## Our Guiding Principle

Identity is not the purpose of Savitri.

Identity is the foundation that allows trust, discoverability, representation, and coordination to exist.

The goal is simple:

Help the right people find, support, inform, and assist one another when it matters most.
